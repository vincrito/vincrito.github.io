--- SLIDE 9 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Case 2: Technical vs Oncologic Resectability
TITLE: Creating Resectability
BULLET_1: Portal vein embolization (PVE): hypertrophy of future liver remnant
BULLET_2: Total venous deprivation (PVE + HVE): combined portal and hepatic vein occlusion for faster hypertrophy
BULLET_3: Two-stage hepatectomy: staged resections when one-stage remnant is insufficient
BULLET_4: ALPPS: rapid bilobar clearance
BULLET_5: Ablation: parenchymal-sparing option for lesions < 3cm
# CHIPS: adam-2004, ligro-2024

--- SLIDE 10 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Case 2: Technical vs Oncologic Resectability
TITLE: Technical vs Oncologic Resectability
BULLET_1: Technical: adequate remnant, achievable margins, patient tolerates the operation
BULLET_2: Oncologic: biology suggests meaningful and durable benefit from resection
BULLET_3: In 2026, most patients are technically resectable. The question should always be oncologic.
BULLET_4: Surgical techniques solve the anatomy problem. Not the biology problem.

--- SLIDE 19 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Timing: Surgery vs Chemotherapy
TITLE: Team Surgery First
BULLET_1: Avoid chemotherapy hepatotoxicity: sinusoidal obstruction syndrome, oxaliplatin neuropathy
BULLET_2: Avoid disappearing lesions: hard to find on imaging, harder to localize intraoperatively
BULLET_3: No risk of progression converting resectable to unresectable (?)
# CHIPS: eortc-2008

--- SLIDE 20 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Timing: Surgery vs Chemotherapy
TITLE: Team Chemotherapy First
BULLET_1: Window to assess biology: progression on therapy is a stop rule before a major operation
BULLET_2: Downstage marginally resectable disease to create a safer resection
BULLET_3: Treat occult micrometastatic disease early
BULLET_4: Identify the responders for whom surgery will matter most
# CHIPS: eortc-2008, adam-2004

--- SLIDE 21 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Timing: Surgery vs Chemotherapy
TITLE: EORTC 40983
BODY: Perioperative FOLFOX vs surgery alone in resectable CRLM.
BULLET_1: PFS improved: ~36% vs 28% at 3 years (HR ~0.77)
BULLET_2: OS: no statistically significant difference at long-term follow-up
BULLET_3: Surgical complication rate higher in the perioperative chemotherapy arm
# CHIPS: eortc-2008, eortc-2013

--- SLIDE 22 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Timing: Surgery vs Chemotherapy
TITLE: JCOG0603
BODY: Hepatectomy + adjuvant mFOLFOX6 vs hepatectomy alone.
BULLET_1: DFS improved: 49.8 vs 24.9 months (HR 0.56)
BULLET_2: OS: not significantly improved

--- SLIDE 23 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Timing: Surgery vs Chemotherapy
TITLE: New EPOC
BODY: Perioperative chemotherapy with or without cetuximab in RAS WT resectable CRLM.
BULLET_1: OS: 55 months with cetuximab vs 81 months without - a meaningful harm
BULLET_2: Cetuximab improves response in unresectable disease; worsens outcomes in resectable disease
BULLET_3: Do not extrapolate across disease stages / treatment settings

TITLE: My Timing Algorithm
BULLET_1: Surgery first: clearly resectable, low-risk biology, concern for disappearing lesions
BULLET_2: Chemotherapy first: biology uncertain, marginal resectability, high tumor burden, need to test response

--- SLIDE 25 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Local Therapy: Ablation and HAI
TITLE: Local Therapy Goals
SUBTITLE: Match the modality to the goal.
BULLET_1: Curative intent: resection when anatomy and biology allow
BULLET_2: Conversion: chemotherapy with or without HAI to downstage, then reassess
BULLET_3: Liver control: ablation, HAI, or both for liver-dominant disease
BULLET_4: Parenchymal sparing: ablation for small peripheral lesions in patients with limited remnant
BULLET_5: Poor PS: ablation, radiation therapy, Y90 if poor surgical tolerance anticipated

TITLE: COLLISION Trial
BODY: Randomized trial of thermal ablation vs surgical resection for CRLM up to 3 cm.
BULLET_1: OS: non-inferior for ablation in size-matched lesions
BULLET_2: Local recurrence higher with ablation, but salvageable in most cases
BULLET_3: Does not mean ablation equals resection for all CRLM: it means ablation is reasonable for small peripheral lesions where remnant is the constraint
# CHIPS: collision-2025

--- SLIDE 28 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Local Therapy: Ablation and HAI
TITLE: Why HAI Chemotherapy?
BODY: The liver is the dominant site of failure in CRLM. HAI delivers floxuridine (FUDR) at concentrations 10-400x higher than IV administration, via the hepatic artery, with lower systemic toxicity.
BULLET_1: First-pass extraction: high local drug concentration, lower systemic exposure
BULLET_2: Always combined with systemic therapy
BULLET_3: Most useful in liver-dominant disease where liver control is the limiting factor for survival
# CHIPS: calgb-9481

--- SLIDE 29 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Local Therapy: Ablation and HAI
TITLE: HAI Data
BULLET_1: CALGB 9481 (RCT): response rate 47% vs 24% (p<0.001); median OS 24.4 vs 20.0 months
BULLET_2: Groot Koerkamp 2017 (n=2,368, propensity matched): median OS 67 vs 44 months; 10-year OS 38% vs 24%
BULLET_3: Effect persisted in the era of modern systemic chemotherapy
BULLET_4: Strongest benefit in low clinical risk score and node-negative primary
# CHIPS: calgb-9481, koerkamp-2017

--- SLIDE 30 | type:slide ---
SECTION: Local Therapy: Ablation and HAI
TITLE: Conversion with HAI: The Selection Question
BODY: D'Angelica, Correa-Gallego et al. Ann Surg 2015
BULLET_1: Median OS and PFS: 38 and 13 months
BULLET_2: 47% conversion rate / 3-year OS 80% vs 26% in resected vs non-resected patients
BULLET_3: We do not know those patients did well because of the surgery
BULLET_4: Surgery may be a marker of favorable biology, not the cause of survival
